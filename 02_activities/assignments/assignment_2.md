## Assignment 2: Good and Bad Data Visualization

### Requirements:

- Data visualizations are important tools for communication and convincing; we need to be able to evaluate the ways that data are presented in visual form to be critical consumers of information 
- To test your evaluation skills, locate two public data visualizations online, one good and one bad  
    - You can find data visualizations at https://public.tableau.com/app/discover or https://datavizproject.com/, or anywhere else you like! 
- For each visualization (good and bad):  
    - Explain (with reference to material covered up to date, along with readings and other scholarly sources, as needed) why you classified that visualization the way you did.
      ```
      Good Visualization
      An example of bad visualization is the one shown at https://www.behance.net/gallery/18012809/Ales-Agglomration-Evolution-de-la-population/modules/122184355
      Reasons:
      1. There is clean typography and spacing, years and numbers are clearly separated from the wedges, minimizing overlap and making labels easy to read.
      2. The plot shows a smooth light-to-dark blue ramp encodes increasing population, helping viewers perceive long‑term growth without reading every label.
      3. The concentric circles mark population levels (70K, 80K, 90K, etc.), making it possible to estimate values and see growth magnitudes accurately
      
      Bad Visualization
      An example of bad visualization is the one shown at https://litoole.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/02/gdppopulation_radialconvergencediagram.jpg
      Reasons:
      1. The author has overplotted lines and nodes create heavy visual clutter, so it is hard to trace any single country’s path or compare between countries.
      2. Using radial layout with many spokes makes length and angle makes interpretation of the plot difficult, which harms accurate reading of GDP or population magnitudes.
      3. Central convergence of lines causes overlaps at the center, obscuring values and making it almost impossible to see which line belongs to which label.
      ```
    - How could this data visualization have been improved?  
      ```
      Good Visualization
      1. Add a short annotation such as “+24K residents since 1968” can be added to concisely state the trend and help non‑technical viewers interpret the graphic.
      2. Arrange years in a perfectly sequential clockwise order and label the direction (e.g., "Time -> clockwise") so temporal progression is unambiguous.
      3. Even a different plot such as heirarchical or organization plot could be tried.

      Bad Visualization
      1. The number of connections can be reduced by filtering to a subset (e.g., top 20 countries, one year, or strongest relationships) or aggregating small countries into groups.
      2. Multiple small charts (e.g., one for GDP, one for population) or a matrix of simpler plots instead of encoding everything in a single radial network can be used to convey the information clearly.
      3. Scale can be adjusted for line colors and node markers and legends can be adjusted for GDP and population, so viewers can interpret values rather than just seeing a tangle of lines.
      ```
- Word count should not exceed (as a maximum) 500 words for each visualization (i.e. 
300 words for your good example and 500 for your bad example)
